The problem

The way most practices collect balances wastes time and money.

Statements take weeks. Every mailed one costs money. Collection agencies keep a big share of what they recover. And the whole process runs on staff hours the practice cannot spare.

01 · SLOW

30 to 45 days per statement

Every statement, mailed or electronic, takes at least 30 days before the patient pays. Many wait 45 days. Some never pay at all.

02 · EXPENSIVE

$0.50 to $1.00 per statement

Every statement costs money to print and mail. Most patients still don’t pay after the first one. So the practice sends another. And another.

03 · COSTLY LATE

Agencies keep 25% to 50%

When a balance gets sent to a collection agency, the agency keeps between a quarter and half of whatever the patient eventually pays.

04 · MANUAL

Hours of staff time every week

Your front desk spends hours a week calling patients and reprinting statements. When the practice grows, the workload grows with it.

Patient journey

Four steps. A card only gets charged after the patient sees a notice.

Every patient gets a chance to pay on their own first. Only patients who never respond move on to the card-on-file step.

01

Pull the list of unpaid balances

Collections Agent reads unpaid balances straight from your EHR. Patients who are already on a payment plan are left out automatically.

02

Send a text with a payment link

Patients get a text with a secure link they can tap to pay. When they don’t respond, the next reminder is sent at a time that works better for them.

03

Check if a card is on file

Only for patients who never responded to the reminders. Nobody’s card is looked at before then.

04

Charge the card, or escalate

If a card is on file, charge it after sending the patient a notice. The few balances that still don’t get paid go to a collection agency. Every step is written down.

Guarantee
No patient’s card is ever looked at until they have had a chance to pay on their own. Cards only get charged after the reminders have gone unanswered, and never without a 24-hour notice.

Why allergy & asthma

Immunotherapy visits create lots of small balances. That is exactly what Collections Agent does best on.

Immunotherapy is a volume specialty.

Patients come in every one or two weeks for allergy shots. Each visit creates a small copay or coinsurance balance. Chasing these by phone doesn’t make sense. Automating them does.

Small balances also get paid at a much higher rate than large ones. That makes automation especially valuable in a volume specialty like allergy: every extra visit is another small balance that would otherwise take staff time to collect.

For older balances that have been sitting for months or years, the practice can offer the patient a discount to close them out. Some money is better than none.
